Smart Maldive Fish Processing

An integrated research project applying AI and IoT to quality assessment and process monitoring in traditional fish processing.

This project explores how computer vision, gas sensing and IoT telemetry can support fish inspection, process optimization and end-to-end production traceability.

Research problem

Why smart Maldive fish processing?

Subjective freshness assessment

Visual checks can vary between assessors and batches.

Uncontrolled boiling

Manual timing can lead to inconsistent processing and energy use.

Unmonitored drying

Changing temperature, humidity and airflow affect drying conditions.

Inconsistent final quality

Final product grading can be subjective without recorded quality data.

Project domain

Research background and objectives

An integrated AI and IoT approach to improve quality assessment, process monitoring and traceability across traditional Maldive fish production.

Literature survey

Prior work in fish freshness assessment, computer vision, food-process monitoring and shelf-life modelling informs the proposed system. See the academic references.

Research problem

Traditional Maldive fish processing relies on subjective inspection and limited monitoring, contributing to variation in process control and final product quality.

Research objectives

Develop an integrated workflow for AI-assisted fish assessment, monitored processing, drying analytics and traceable quality evaluation.

Research gap

Many existing approaches address a single stage. This project brings inspection, process monitoring and final quality assessment together in one workflow.

Project objectives

  • Assess raw fish freshness using computer vision and gas-sensor readings.
  • Monitor and optimize boiling, salting and drying conditions.
  • Evaluate final product quality using image-based classification.
  • Keep a traceable record of batch and process information.

Research pillars

AI quality inspection

Vision-driven freshness assessment for raw fish using object detection and sensor fusion.

Thermal process control

Boiling and salting are optimized through empirical formulas and thermal response.

Drying telemetry

ESP32 nodes monitor humidity, airflow and VOC signals to support a stable environment.

Predictive analytics

Shelf-life modelling and grade prediction support a measurable production loop.

Research pipeline

Eight-stage processing workflow

  1. Raw fish intakeBatch registration and initial sensory screening for fresh tuna intake.
  2. AI freshness inspectionYOLO11s detection and freshness classification from images and gas readings. mAP@0.5: 95.8%.
  3. Intelligent boilingAdjust boiling using thickness, fish mass and freshness-aware thermal models.
  4. Brine saltingTarget 12%–15% brine concentration and improve salt uniformity.
  5. IoT drying monitoringESP32 nodes stream temperature, humidity, airflow and VOC trends.
  6. Final product assessmentGrade dried product using ResNet-18 and sensor-derived quality signals.
  7. Shelf-life predictionArrhenius-based model estimates storage reliability and product longevity.
  8. Digital storage traceabilityRetain a digital trail for batch process history and quality review.
